# NYSDEC DER-10 Technical Guidance for Site Investigation and Remediation

> **Note:** DER-10 is a guidance document, not a regulation. It is not codified in 6 NYCRR but is the primary interpretive tool used by NYSDEC project managers and environmental professionals to evaluate site conditions under the Spill Program and other remediation programs administered by the Division of Environmental Remediation (DER).

---

## Purpose

DER-10 provides technical guidance for conducting site investigations, evaluating contamination, and implementing remedial actions at sites subject to NYSDEC oversight under:
- Environmental Conservation Law (ECL) Article 17, Title 10 (Petroleum Bulk Storage / Spill Program)
- ECL Article 27, Title 13 (Inactive Hazardous Waste Disposal Sites / State Superfund)
- ECL Article 27, Title 14 (Environmental Restoration Program)
- ECL Article 56 (Environmental Restoration Programs — Brownfield Cleanup Program)

DER-10 guides how NYSDEC project managers classify sites, evaluate remedial alternatives, and determine when cleanup objectives have been met. For Phase 1 ESA purposes, the most relevant provisions concern how Spill Program and State Superfund records are classified and what closure statuses mean in terms of REC/CREC/HREC classification.

---

## Preliminary Assessment Triggers

A Preliminary Assessment (PA) is initiated by NYSDEC under the Spill Program when:
1. A spill is reported to the NYSDEC Spill Hotline (800-457-7362) under ECL §17-1743
2. A release is discovered during agency inspections or third-party referrals
3. A SEQRA review, subdivision approval, or other permitting process triggers environmental review
4. A citizen complaint identifies suspected contamination
5. EDR database records identify unreported historical releases

**PA relevance to Phase 1 REC classification:** An open NYSDEC Spill Program record (active PA) at or near the subject property constitutes a REC until closed with appropriate documentation.

## Spill Program Record Classifications

### Classification System
NYSDEC classifies inactive hazardous waste sites (State Superfund) on a numeric scale and Spill Program records by case status. The classifications most frequently encountered in EDR reports and their Phase 1 implications are:

| NYSDEC Classification | Description | Typical Phase 1 Classification |
|---|---|---|
| **Class 1** | Significant threat to public health or environment; immediate action needed | REC |
| **Class 2** | Significant threat; remedial program underway or required | REC |
| **Class 3** | No significant threat; monitoring may be required | REC (EP judgment — may be de minimis depending on conditions) |
| **Class 4** | Closed — site closed, cleanup completed | HREC (if unrestricted use SCOs met without controls) or CREC (if controls remain) |
| **Class 5** | Superfund equivalent — federal NPL listing considered or pursued | REC |
| **Open — Active** | Spill Program record: investigation or remediation in progress | REC |
| **Open — Inactive** | Spill Program record: case open but no active remediation | REC |
| **Closed — No Further Action (NFAR)** | Regulatory agency has issued No Further Action Required determination | HREC (if no controls) or CREC (if institutional/engineering controls remain) |
| **Closed — Case Closed** | Administrative closure; may or may not reflect complete cleanup | EP judgment required — review closure documentation |
| **Closed — Referred** | Case referred to another regulatory program | REC pending disposition |

## Closure Status Definitions

### No Further Action Required (NFAR)
A NFAR determination means NYSDEC has concluded that:
- The site has been adequately investigated;
- Contamination has been addressed to meet applicable cleanup objectives (SCOs); and
- No further remedial action is required under the applicable program.

**NFAR implications for Phase 1 classification:**
- NFAR with unrestricted use SCOs met and no controls remaining → **HREC**
- NFAR with institutional controls (e.g., deed restriction on groundwater use, engineered cap) → **CREC**
- NFAR issued under prior, less-stringent cleanup standards → EP must evaluate whether current SCOs are exceeded; may need to treat as CREC or REC

### Closed — Case Closed
Administrative closure may occur when:
- Contamination is below detection limits after remediation
- Contamination is below reporting thresholds
- The responsible party cannot be located (does not imply cleanup was completed)

**Note:** "Closed — Case Closed" does NOT always mean cleanup was completed to SCOs. Review available documentation before classifying as HREC.

### Open Spill Records
Any open NYSDEC Spill Program record (regardless of apparent activity level) at the subject property or within the standard search radius constitutes a potential REC until closed with appropriate documentation.

## Key Classification Guidance for Phase 1 ESA Writers

### 1. Petroleum Spill Records with NFAR Closure
- If a petroleum release has received an NFAR letter and no institutional controls remain, classify as **HREC**
- If residual petroleum contamination remains at concentrations above unrestricted use SCOs but below restricted use SCOs, and an engineering control (cap) is in place, classify as **CREC**
- If the NFAR was issued many years ago and there is reason to believe migration has continued, note this and recommend verification

### 2. Institutional Controls / Engineering Controls
- Sites with active deed restrictions, activity and use limitations (AULs), or engineered caps classify as **CREC**, not HREC
- Review available regulatory documents to identify any active institutional or engineering controls
- IC/EC Registries should be searched as part of government records review

### 3. Off-Site Source Proximity
- An off-site source (upgradient or adjacent) constitutes a REC for the subject property if:
  - The source has documented contamination that may have migrated or could reasonably migrate to the subject property
  - The groundwater flow direction places the subject property in the downgradient or crossgradient position
  - The distance and separation are insufficient to preclude migration

### 4. Prior Regulatory Standards
- If a site was closed under cleanup standards that are less stringent than current Part 375 SCOs, the EP must evaluate whether current SCOs are likely exceeded
- If current SCO exceedance is likely and no controls remain, classification may need to be elevated from HREC to REC or CREC

### 5. Vapor Intrusion Pathway
- NYSDEC now requires evaluation of the vapor intrusion (VI) pathway for sites with VOC contamination
- An open VOC source (chlorinated solvents, petroleum hydrocarbons) within the VI proximity criteria (typically 100 feet for chlorinated solvents; 100 feet downgradient for petroleum) constitutes a REC via the VI pathway even if direct soil/groundwater contact with the subject property is not confirmed
- Cross-reference with NYSDOH vapor intrusion guidance (see Law_State_NYSDOH_Vapor.md)

## Brownfield Cleanup Program (BCP) and VCP

- **BCP (ECL Article 56):** Voluntary program for redevelopment of brownfield sites. BCP participation and issuance of a Certificate of Completion (COC) with unrestricted use cleanup → HREC. COC with institutional controls → CREC.
- **VCP (Voluntary Cleanup Program):** Sites with known or suspected contamination that do not qualify for BCP. VCP completion with NFAR → similar analysis to Spill Program NFAR.
- **Environmental Restoration Program (ERP):** Municipally-driven cleanup program. Same HREC/CREC analysis applies upon completion.
