# NESHAP — Asbestos Demolition/Renovation Notification

> Source: National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants (NESHAP), 40 CFR Part 61, Subpart M — National Emission Standard for Asbestos.

---

## Federal NESHAP (40 CFR Part 61, Subpart M)

### Purpose
The federal Asbestos NESHAP establishes emission standards and work practice standards for asbestos demolition and renovation activities, including mandatory advance notification to the applicable regulatory agency before activities begin.

### Definition of RACM
**Regulated Asbestos-Containing Material (RACM):** (40 CFR §61.141)
1. Friable asbestos material;
2. Category I non-friable ACM that has become friable;
3. Category I non-friable ACM that will be or has been subjected to sanding, grinding, cutting, or abrading; or
4. Category II non-friable ACM that has a high probability of becoming or has become crumbled, pulverized, or reduced to powder by the forces expected to act on the material in the course of demolition or renovation operations.

### Applicability Thresholds (40 CFR §61.145)
Advance notification and work practice requirements apply when RACM to be disturbed equals or exceeds any one of:
- **≥260 linear feet** on pipes
- **≥160 square feet** on other facility components
- **≥35 cubic feet** off facility components where length or area could not be measured previously

### Advance Notification Requirement
- Written notice must be submitted to the appropriate EPA Regional Office (or delegated state agency) **at least 10 working days before the start of demolition or renovation work**
- Emergency demolitions: as soon as possible, no later than the following working day
- Notice must include: facility owner/operator, location, description of demolition/renovation, amount of RACM, scheduled start and completion dates, waste disposal site information, and name/address of asbestos removal contractor

### Wet Methods and Disposal
- RACM must be kept wet during removal, collection, and packaging
- RACM waste must be sealed in leak-tight containers or bags clearly labeled: "DANGER: CONTAINS ASBESTOS FIBERS. AVOID CREATING DUST. CANCER AND LUNG DISEASE HAZARD"
- Disposal only at sites authorized to accept RACM waste

## New York State — Delegation and Implementing Regulations

### EPA Delegation
New York State has received EPA delegation of the federal Asbestos NESHAP program. Regulatory oversight is shared between:
- **NYSDEC** (air emissions component): 6 NYCRR Part 226 — Asbestos emission standards
- **NYSDOL Asbestos Control Bureau (ACB)**: 12 NYCRR Part 56 — Asbestos project certification, contractor licensing, and notification requirements for all asbestos projects in New York regardless of NESHAP thresholds

### NYSDOL 12 NYCRR Part 56 — Key Provisions
- All asbestos abatement projects in New York State require notification to NYSDOL ACB, regardless of quantity (lower thresholds than federal NESHAP)
- Asbestos contractors must be licensed by NYSDOL; workers must be certified
- Air monitoring by a third-party industrial hygienist required during abatement
- Post-abatement clearance air testing required before re-occupancy
- Applies to all friable ACM removal in any commercial, industrial, institutional, or residential building with more than 4 dwelling units

### 6 NYCRR Part 226 — Air Emission Standards
New York State asbestos emission standards equivalent to federal NESHAP standards; administered by NYSDEC Division of Air Resources.

## Phase 1 ESA Relevance

### When to Note NESHAP Issues in a Phase 1 Report
- If pre-demolition records or site reconnaissance suggest prior asbestos abatement was conducted on the subject property, note in the Site Reconnaissance and/or Historical Records section
- If ACM or RACM appears to have been improperly disposed of on the subject property (e.g., visible debris containing ACM in soil or fill), this constitutes a potential **REC** (improper waste disposal)
- If asbestos abatement was conducted and is documented as complete with clearance testing passed, note as BER — not a REC
- If a building on the subject property is scheduled for renovation or demolition and contains suspect ACM above NESHAP thresholds, note NESHAP notification requirements as a BER item

### REC vs. BER Classification Guidance
| Condition | Classification |
|---|---|
| ACM in intact, good condition in existing building | BER (not a REC) |
| ACM observed in debris/soil (possible improper disposal) | Potential REC — EP judgment required |
| Prior asbestos abatement, properly completed and documented | BER (not a REC) |
| Prior asbestos abatement, documentation unavailable | Data gap — note and recommend verification |
| Planned demolition/renovation of ACM-containing structure | BER — notify NYSDOL, NESHAP may apply |

## Suggested Report Language

**For BER notation of in-place ACM:**
> "The [building name/description] at the subject property was constructed in [year], and may contain asbestos-containing materials (ACM) in thermal system insulation, surfacing materials, and/or miscellaneous building materials. If renovation or demolition activities are planned, a pre-demolition asbestos survey should be conducted by a licensed asbestos inspector, and any RACM must be abated in accordance with 40 CFR Part 61 Subpart M (NESHAP) and 12 NYCRR Part 56 (NYSDOL) prior to disturbance. This condition is considered a Business Environmental Risk (BER) item pursuant to ASTM E1527-21 §13."

**For potential REC (improper disposal):**
> "During the site reconnaissance, [describe observation — e.g., debris containing suspect asbestos-containing pipe insulation] was observed [location on property]. Improper disposal of RACM on the subject property is considered a Recognized Environmental Condition (REC) pursuant to ASTM E1527-21. A Phase 2 ESA including asbestos waste characterization is recommended."
