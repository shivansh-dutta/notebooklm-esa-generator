# TSCA — Asbestos and Lead Paint (Phase 1 ESA Scope)

> Note: Asbestos-containing materials (ACM) and lead-based paint (LBP) are **Business Environmental Risk (BER)** items under ASTM E1527-21 §13, not standard Phase 1 ESA scope. They are noted when observed during site reconnaissance and recommended as additional services when triggered. They do NOT constitute RECs unless improper disposal has occurred.

---

## Asbestos (TSCA Title II — AHERA)

### Statutory Authority
Asbestos Hazard Emergency Response Act (AHERA), TSCA Title II, 15 U.S.C. §§ 2641–2656. Implementing regulations at 40 CFR Part 763.

### Definition of ACM
**Asbestos-Containing Material (ACM):** Any material or product that contains more than one percent (1%) asbestos by weight (40 CFR §763.83).

**Friable ACM:** ACM that, when dry, can be crumbled, pulverized, or reduced to powder by hand pressure. Friable ACM poses the greatest risk of fiber release and is subject to the most stringent handling and disposal requirements.

### Building Types of Concern
- Pre-1980 construction: high likelihood of ACM in thermal system insulation (TSI), surfacing materials (spray-applied fireproofing, plaster), and miscellaneous materials (floor tiles, ceiling tiles, roofing, gaskets, pipe wrap)
- Pre-1981 schools: subject to mandatory AHERA inspection and management plan requirements under 40 CFR Part 763

### Phase 1 ESA Relevance
- During site reconnaissance, the EP should visually note the age and construction type of buildings and the apparent presence of suspect ACM (e.g., insulated pipes, spray-applied ceilings, floor tiles)
- If suspect ACM is observed, note in the Site Reconnaissance section as a BER item
- Recommend an asbestos survey by a licensed asbestos inspector as an additional service prior to renovation or demolition
- ACM in good condition (non-friable, not disturbed) does NOT constitute a REC
- Improperly disposed ACM on the subject property (e.g., dumped demolition debris containing ACM) MAY constitute a REC depending on quantity and condition

### Standard Report Language (BER Notation)
> "During the site reconnaissance, [describe observation — e.g., insulated mechanical systems and floor tiles consistent with asbestos-containing materials (ACM)] were observed within the [building/structure]. The presence of asbestos-containing materials in buildings is considered a Business Environmental Risk (BER) item pursuant to ASTM E1527-21 §13 and is beyond the standard scope of this Phase 1 ESA. An asbestos survey conducted by a licensed asbestos inspector is recommended prior to any renovation or demolition activities."

## Lead-Based Paint (TSCA Title IV — §§ 402/403/404)

### Statutory Authority
TSCA Title IV, 15 U.S.C. §§ 2681–2692. Primary implementing regulations: 40 CFR Part 745 (Lead; Requirements for Lead-Based Paint Activities in Target Housing and Child-Occupied Facilities).

### Definition of LBP
**Lead-Based Paint (LBP):** Paint or other surface coatings that contain lead in excess of **0.5 percent** (5,000 ppm) lead by weight, or **1.0 milligram per square centimeter (mg/cm²)** as determined by XRF analysis (40 CFR §745.223).

### Applicability
- **Pre-1978 residential and commercial buildings**: Most likely to contain LBP. The use of lead in residential paint was banned effective January 1, 1978.
- **EPA RRP Rule (40 CFR §745.80)**: Renovation, Repair, and Painting Rule — requires lead-safe work practices for renovation activities in pre-1978 target housing and child-occupied facilities.
- **HUD Guidelines**: Additional requirements for federally assisted housing.

### Phase 1 ESA Relevance
- Note the age of structures on the subject property during site reconnaissance
- Pre-1978 structures: recommend LBP survey as additional service, especially if renovation is planned
- Deteriorating, peeling, or chipping paint in pre-1978 buildings should be noted as a BER item
- LBP that has been properly encapsulated or enclosure-stabilized does NOT constitute a REC
- If LBP disturbance has resulted in lead contamination of soils around the building perimeter (drip zone), this MAY constitute a REC depending on concentrations and property use

### Standard Report Language (BER Notation)
> "The subject property includes structures constructed prior to 1978. Pre-1978 buildings may contain lead-based paint (LBP). The presence of LBP is considered a Business Environmental Risk (BER) item pursuant to ASTM E1527-21 §13 and is beyond the standard scope of this Phase 1 ESA. An LBP inspection and/or risk assessment conducted by a licensed LBP inspector is recommended prior to any renovation or demolition activities."
