# NYSDOH Vapor Intrusion Guidance

> **IMPORTANT:** NYSDOH vapor intrusion guidance values are periodically revised. Verify current screening values at nyhealth.gov/environmental/investigations/soil_gas/ before citing specific numbers in a report. The framework and pathway triggers below are stable; the numeric thresholds are subject to update.

---

## Purpose and Regulatory Standing

NYSDOH vapor intrusion (VI) guidance is not a regulation but is widely adopted by NYSDEC and the regulated community as the standard of practice for evaluating the VI pathway in New York. The guidance establishes:
- Screening criteria to determine when VI evaluation is warranted
- Indoor air and sub-slab soil gas screening values (Hazard Index Levels / HALs)
- Protocols for sub-slab and indoor air sampling
- Risk-based cleanup criteria for VI pathway

**Relationship to Phase 1 ESA:** Vapor intrusion is a recognized environmental condition pathway under ASTM E1527-21. A nearby VOC source (open or recently closed) may constitute a REC for the subject property specifically through the VI pathway, even if direct soil or groundwater contact with the subject property is not confirmed.

---

## Investigation Triggers

A VI investigation is triggered when all three of the following conditions exist:

1. **VOC Source:** Known or suspected subsurface VOC contamination exists in soil or groundwater (chlorinated solvents, petroleum hydrocarbons with aromatic components, or other volatile contaminants)

2. **Proximity to Occupied Structure:** The source is within the following approximate proximity thresholds (NYSDOH guidance):
   - **Chlorinated solvents (TCE, PCE, etc.):** Source within **~100 feet** of occupied building, or groundwater plume within proximity that could migrate toward building
   - **Petroleum hydrocarbons (BTEX):** Petroleum release within **~100 feet** of occupied building (note: fresh petroleum releases are typically more relevant than old, biodegraded releases)
   - **Distance thresholds are guidelines**, not bright lines — engineering judgment required

3. **Building Characteristics:** The building has characteristics that increase VI susceptibility:
   - Basement or below-grade space
   - Slab-on-grade construction
   - Crawl space
   - Utility conduit entry points below grade

**Phase 1 ESA Application:** If an open or recently closed VOC source exists within the proximity thresholds above and the subject property has a structure, the EP should note the VI pathway as a REC and recommend sub-slab vapor sampling as a Phase 2 additional service.

## Phase 1 ESA Relevance

### 1. When a VOC Source Constitutes a REC via VI Pathway
A nearby VOC contamination source constitutes a REC for the subject property through the VI pathway when:
- The source is within the proximity thresholds described above
- The subject property has a building with basement, slab-on-grade, or crawl space construction
- The source is open (active remediation or monitoring) OR recently closed with residual VOC concentrations
- Example: Dry cleaner site (PCE source) within 100 feet upgradient of the subject property with documented groundwater contamination → **REC** for subject property via VI pathway

### 2. Documentation in Findings and Opinions
The EP should:
- State the distance and direction of the VOC source from the subject property
- Note the building foundation type at the subject property
- Note whether groundwater flow direction places the subject property in the potential influence zone
- Classify as REC and recommend sub-slab vapor sampling as a Phase 2 activity

### 3. CREC Designation for Sites with Active VI Controls
If a closed source has a sub-slab depressurization (SSD) system, vapor barrier, or other active VI mitigation system in place:
- This constitutes a **CREC** — the condition has been addressed with engineering controls that must remain effective
- Not an HREC, because the control system must be maintained
- Note the presence and operational status of the VI control system

### 4. VI as a Data Gap
If sub-surface data is unavailable but a VOC source exists within proximity:
- Note as a **data gap** when soil/groundwater data is insufficient to evaluate the VI pathway
- Recommend Phase 2 sub-slab soil vapor sampling to evaluate VI potential
- Example language: "Insufficient subsurface data are available to fully evaluate the vapor intrusion pathway associated with [source name]. This represents a data gap. Sub-slab soil vapor sampling is recommended as part of a Phase 2 ESA."

### 5. Cross-Reference with DER-10
- NYSDEC DER-10 requires VI pathway evaluation for sites with VOC contamination
- An NFAR closure at a VOC site does not preclude VI concerns if a VI control system remains active (CREC)
- Verify that VI pathway was specifically addressed in the regulatory closure documentation before classifying as HREC
